## Owner signal

Tax day is done. The next decisions are about cash left in the account, what money will cost, and who covers the phone when you are on a job.

August CPI came in hotter on the month (BLS: +0.4% seasonally adjusted; +3.4% over the year). Gasoline rose +3.9% on the month and did a large share of that monthly move. Energy is still the loud part of the inflation story (+2.1% on the month; +16.3% over the year).

The Fed met Sep 15 to 16. Going in, the funds rate was 3.50% to 3.75%. On Sep 16 the FOMC raised the target range by 25 basis points to 3.75% to 4.00%, on a 12-0 vote. Do not refinance or load a big equipment note on a hoped-for cut. Price credit at the new range.

Main Street sales were already soft in the August NFIB reading (net -9% on recent sales). Hiring plans cooled to net 17% planning to add jobs, while openings stay hard to fill. Optimism slipped to 98.7 but stayed above the long-run average.

Thesis: protect cash after the Sep 15 payment. Audit ad waste. Hire only after coverage is measured. Patch Windows this week.

## Hiring, coverage, and AI Max mid-month

Two different problems get mixed up: not enough qualified people, and not enough answer rate on the leads you already pay for.

On hiring: NFIB still shows owners with unfilled openings and few qualified applicants. If you hire, hire for a measured gap (booked jobs you are turning away), not because the inbox feels busy.

On ads: Google's September AI Max migration for Search campaigns that used Automatically Created Assets or campaign-level broad match is mid-flight (Sep 1 to 30). Dynamic Search Ads are on a later clock (February 2027), not this month. Local risk is the same as last issue, only more real now: broader matching, AI-written text, spend on queries you never meant to buy, and front-desk time spent correcting the ad.

This week's ops checklist:

1. Export the last 7 to 14 days of search terms on every Search campaign. Add negatives for out-of-area, DIY, and services you do not sell.
2. Spot-check ad text and landing pages. If the ad invents a price, service, or city, pause that asset path and fix it.
3. Optimize to a booked call or appointment, not a click.
4. Before you post another job: log missed calls and after-hours inquiries for one week. If coverage is the leak, fix that first. A new hire will not answer a call that never reached a human or a system.

AI used well here means answering, booking, and following up. AI used poorly means writing ads that spend your money and burn trust.

Fuel and materials: gasoline's August jump is already showing up in trip costs. Re-check trip fees, minimums, and surcharge language on estimates before the next busy weekend.

## Security: Windows Update before Sep 22

Skip the CVE laundry list. What matters: Microsoft's Sep 8 Patch Tuesday fixed two Windows flaws already being exploited in the wild (CVE-2026-81963 and CVE-2026-85880). CISA put both on the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities list with a Sep 22 remediation date for federal agencies. Treat that as your shop's deadline too.

Also in that release: a Windows SMB Client remote code execution fix (CVE-2026-72936). It is Important and worth installing, especially on PCs and servers that use file shares. It is not listed as exploited in the wild and is not on the KEV list. Still install the September cumulative updates.

Plain English checklist:

1. Run Windows Update on every work PC and server this week. Restart when asked. Do not wait for a slow staged rollout.
2. Ask your MSP to confirm the Sep 8 cumulative updates are installed, especially on machines that hold books, customer files, or VPN access.
3. Keep MFA on email, VPN, banking, and payroll.
4. Confirm an offline or immutable backup exists. Test restore of one critical folder.
5. Chrome, Edge, and Brave: stay on the latest Stable build. Help, About, update, restart.

If something looks wrong after a patch (odd login prompts, locked files, ransom note), do not negotiate alone. Call your MSP, IC3/FBI, or CISA (1-844-Say-CISA).
